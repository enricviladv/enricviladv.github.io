## Job Market Paper 

 Social Preferences as Human Capital: Intergenerational Transmission in an Ethnically Diverse Context

  <details>
  <summary><strong>Abstract</strong></summary>

  Prosociality is essential for cooperation and the functioning of human societies, and is associated with important economic outcomes such as income, labor market prospects, or welfare. Social preferences are a key determinant of prosocial behavior. Yet, despite their central role in shaping behavior, relatively little is known about how these preferences are formed and what factors shape them. In this paper, I study how ethnic identity and parental influence are associated with social preferences. To do so, I elicit social preferences across 842 parent-child pairs from both ethnic majority and Roma minority in Bulgaria using survey- and task-based items. The results indicate that prosocial behavior and a preference for the in-group are common in this context and are positively associated between generations. I also find that in-group preferences are driven by a relatively small number of individuals across groups, as a majority of parents and children do not display an in-group or out-group preference. I also explore potential mechanisms associated with the intergenerational correlation of social preferences. In ethnic majority households, positive stereotypes, daily contact with the out-group, and a prosocial parenting style are associated with the intergenerational correlation of social preferences. Conversely, in ethnic Roma households, only a prosocial parenting style is associated with this intergenerational correlation.


  </details>
